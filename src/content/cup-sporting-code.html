1. Introduction

The GRR Cup Series is committed to fair, competitive, and enjoyable racing. Every participant must follow this Sporting Code. Violations may result in penalties.

Rules may change. Drivers are responsible for staying current with all updates.

2. General Conduct

2.1 Fair Play

Drivers must race fairly, respectfully, and professionally. Reckless or needlessly aggressive driving will not be tolerated.

Intentional wrecking or unsportsmanlike conduct may result in disqualification or suspension.

2.2 Responsibility

Drivers are responsible for their conduct on track and in all league communications.

Unprofessional conduct may result in penalties.

2.3 Communication

Voice and text communication must remain respectful. Harassment or abuse may result in discipline.

Disputes or incidents must be reported according to league procedures. See Section 7, Filing a Protest.

2.4 Eligibility

GRR welcomes drivers of varied skill levels. To maintain racing standards, drivers must meet the following requirements:

  • Drivers who maintain an official iRacing Oval License of C Class and a minimum 1300 iRating are eligible to drive.
  • Drivers who do not maintain a C Class Oval License and 1300 iRating may still be eligible if they qualify within 105% of the pole-position lap time.
  • Approved drivers below the standard requirement enter probation and receive a [PROB] iRacing prefix.
  • Probation ends after five consecutive races within 105% of pole and with no License Points.
  • A top-10 championship finish will also result in the driver exiting probation.
  • Existing drivers are grandfathered under the eligibility rules in effect when they joined. Pre-Code participants are exempt.

3. Season / Race Rules

3.1 Season Format (2026)

Regular Season 26 Weeks

Chase 10 Races

3.2 Chase Format

After the 26-week regular season concludes, the top 16 drivers in the points standings will have their points reset to the values listed in Section 6.4.

The driver with the most points after the 10-race Chase is Champion.

3.3 Race Format

  • Qualifying: Two laps of lone qualifying with 10 minutes to complete.
  • Stages: Two stages. Stage 1 ends at a predetermined distance and a caution will be displayed. Stage 2 ends with the checkered flag.
  • Race Length: 50% of the real-life NASCAR Cup Series counterpart, except marked Crown Jewel races, which run at 100% distance.
  • Tires: Five sets per race.
  • Fast Repairs: One per race.
  • Grid Size: Maximum of 41 cars. If more than 41 drivers enter, the top 30 in points automatically qualify, with remaining positions determined by qualifying. GRR staff members may receive a provisional if needed.
  • Cautions: Assessed by iRacing’s automatic full-course caution system. A staff member will manually trigger a full-course caution only in extreme or unusual circumstances.

3.4 Race Starts

Drivers must hold position until the green flag.

Caution Restarts: After a caution period, the restart is controlled by the race leader. The lead car must hold pace after the safety car enters pit lane and may accelerate anywhere within the starting zone. League staff may extend cautions if the field is not in proper formation.

On all starts and restarts, drivers must remain in their assigned lane until crossing the start/finish line. A violation discovered after the race will result in the driver being scored at the end of the lap on which they finished.

3.5 Appropriate Conduct for Oval Races

Overtaking: The overtaking driver is responsible for being 100% clear of the car being passed.

Defending: A driver may move to block or defend but must do so safely, maintain control, and avoid aggressive weaving or blocking, especially when another car is alongside.

Wall Ride: Intentionally riding the outside wall at full throttle to gain a speed advantage is prohibited and will result in either a drive-through penalty or a 15-second post-race penalty.

3.6 Appropriate Conduct for Road Courses

Overtaking: Passes must be attempted safely, particularly in braking zones and corners. If contact results from an overly aggressive move, the driver initiating the pass may be penalized.

Defending: A driver may make only one defensive move per corner. Aggressive defense that forces another car off track or makes a pass dangerous will not be tolerated.

3.7 Rejoining the Track

After leaving the racing surface, the driver is solely responsible for rejoining safely and under control.

Drivers must yield to traffic. A rejoin that forces evasive action, costs another driver time, or causes an incident will be penalized.

Unsafe rejoins include, but are not limited to:
  • Cutting across the racing line at speed.
  • Pulling directly into traffic.
  • Spinning the tires while rejoining.
  • Rejoining in a blind corner or at a high-speed entry point without full control.

3.8 Yellow Line Rule

If, in GRR’s judgment, a driver goes below the double yellow line to improve position, that driver will be black-flagged. If a driver forces another car below the double yellow line to prevent a pass, the forcing driver may be black-flagged.

Infractions discovered during post-race review may result in position loss, being scored last, or disqualification, depending on the circumstances and severity. If league staff identify the infraction during the race, the driver will be given an opportunity to surrender the position gained.

3.9 Pit Lane Commitment Rule

Once a driver crosses the designated pit-entry commitment line, often painted orange, they must enter pit road and may not return to the racing surface until completing their pass through pit lane.

Drivers are responsible for knowing each track’s pit lane commitment line. An infraction discovered during the race will result in a drive-through penalty. An infraction discovered post-race will result in a 15-second time penalty.

3.10 Race Manipulation Rule

Any member attempting to manipulate the outcome of a race or engaging in actions detrimental to the sport faces severe penalties.

Definition of Manipulation: Any action intended to artificially alter finishing positions or prioritize non-competitive objectives is prohibited.

4. Car Setups / Liveries

4.1 Car Setups

Open setups are permitted within iRacing’s rules. All setups must be legal.

4.2 Car Liveries

  • All liveries must comply with the iRacing Custom Paint Policy.
  • Drivers, not Grass Roots Racing, are entirely responsible for the graphics, content, and paint schemes used on their cars.
  • Paint schemes may not be indecent, libelous, defamatory, obscene, threatening, invasive of privacy or publicity rights, abusive, illegal, harassing, hateful, bigoted, racist, pornographic, promotional of illicit drugs, encouraging of a criminal offense, or otherwise objectionable.
  • Use of Grass Roots Racing-provided contingency overlays is strongly recommended but is not generally required for race participation.
  • Drivers competing in an in-season tournament, playoff bracket, or other promotional event with a special contingency overlay must use that overlay to be eligible. League staff will help any driver who is unable to add it themselves.

5. License Points and Penalties

5.1 License Point Allocation

The League uses License Points (LPs) to track driver behavior during races. LPs are assessed for avoidable incidents or rule violations within the ranges below, at staff discretion. Accumulating too many LPs results in escalating penalties.

Minor Contact / Minor Off-Track

Penalty: 0–1 LP and/or Race Control penalty

Light door rubs, incidental contact that does not cause a spin or affect the race, or a minor off-track with no position gained.

Causing a Caution — No Other Cars Involved

Penalty: 1 LP and/or Race Control penalty

A driver loses control and causes a caution without involving another car.

Causing a Caution or Wreck — Other Cars Involved

Penalty: 1–5 LP and/or Race Control penalty

Unnecessary contact, failure to reasonably brake or avoid a wreck, or an accident involving another car. Severity determines the LP assessment.

Cutting the Track / Gaining Position Unfairly

Penalty: 3–5 LP and/or in-race or post-race penalty

Intentionally taking an off-track shortcut or otherwise gaining an unfair advantage through an off-track excursion.

Intentional Wrecking / Deliberate Dangerous Driving

Penalty: 13–20 LP and/or race suspension

Deliberately spinning another driver or causing a major accident in a manner perceived as intentional.

Unsportsmanlike Conduct

Penalty: 0–25 LP and/or race suspension

Conduct on or off track that does not reflect GRR values. Severity is determined by GRR staff.

7. Filing a Protest

If a driver believes an incident was not properly handled during the race, they may file a protest by creating a ticket in the protest channel of the League’s Discord server.

7.1 Protest Process

  1. Race Stewards and designated staff will review the incident.
  2. The panel considers severity, replay evidence, and race impact.
  3. The decision is final unless new evidence is presented.

8. Teams (Optional)

8.1 Team Composition

  • Each team may consist of up to four full-time drivers.
  • Each team will designate one Team Representative to represent the team’s interests in official league meetings.
  • All drivers on a team must use cars from the same manufacturer throughout the season.
  • A driver may not compete for more than one team in a season without notifying league officials.

8.2 Team Rights

Each team will be given representation in certain league decisions.

8.3 Owner’s Championship

  • Team points are calculated by combining the regular-season points of all drivers on the team.
  • The combined total contributes to the Owner’s Championship, which is scored separately from the Driver’s Championship.
  • Teams with fewer than three drivers still receive Owner’s Championship credit based on their participating members.

8.4 Team Identity

  • Teams are strongly encouraged to display a team logo or identifier on their liveries.
  • Logos must be appropriate, clearly visible, and not conflict with league branding or guidelines.
  • Teams may register their name, logo, and manufacturer with league officials before the season.

8.5 Team Registration

  • Teams must register their name, manufacturer, and driver lineup before the season begins.
  • Changes to team composition or manufacturer after registration require approval from Race Control or the League Administrator.

9. League and Admin Authority

9.1 Race Directors and Stewards

The Race Director and appointed Stewards have full authority to enforce the rules and penalize drivers who violate them.

Their decisions regarding incidents, protests, and race management are final and not subject to appeal.

9.2 Modifications to the Sporting Code

The League reserves the right to modify this Sporting Code at any time. Changes will be communicated through League communication channels, including Discord.

All drivers are responsible for staying informed about rule updates.

10. Conclusion

Participation in the GRR Cup Series means accepting this Sporting Code.

GRR’s goal is clean, competitive racing for every member.
