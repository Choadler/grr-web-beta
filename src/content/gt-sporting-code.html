1. League Philosophy

Grass Roots Racing (GRR) was founded to create a welcoming and competitive environment for drivers of all skill levels. While we strive to provide the highest quality racing possible, our primary goal is to build a community where members enjoy racing together, improve their skills, and develop lasting friendships both on and off the track.

GRR was originally established to provide drivers with an accessible entry point into endurance and multiclass racing. That mission continues today through respectful competition, fair stewardship, and a commitment to sportsmanship.

All league members are expected to compete in a manner that reflects these values and contributes positively to the community.

1.1 Competition Committee

The Competition Committee (CC) is responsible for the administration and operation of Grass Roots Racing. The Committee oversees league management, membership, rule updates, disciplinary actions, stewarding oversight, and any other matters necessary to ensure the continued success of the league. The Competition Committee and appointed stewards have final authority over all league competition matters, including rule interpretations, incident reviews, penalties, classifications, and championship administration.

The Competition Committee consists of the League Founder, League Managers, appointed staff members, and any additional individuals approved by the current committee.

2. General Rules and Formats

2.1 iRacing Sporting Code

All drivers are expected to know and follow the iRacing Sporting Code (when applicable), especially in terms of conduct.

2.2 Voice Chat

All drivers are required to have voice chat enabled so they can hear the Race Control channel (Admins), but are not required to own a mic. Drivers are expected to keep race communications concise and professional during official sessions. Discussion of incidents should be reserved for after the race after all parties have had a chance to view the replay. It is common courtesy to keep the radio clear unless communication is absolutely needed.

2.3 Fast Repair

Unless otherwise specified in the event announcement, each driver will receive one (1) Fast Repair per race session.

2.4 Incident Limit

An incident limit will be in effect for all GRR races. The standard limit is 17 incident points, though Race Control may adjust the limit based on track characteristics, race length, or special event requirements. Endurance races will use a standard limit of 25 incident points unless otherwise noted.

2.4.1 Penalties for Exceeding Incident Limit

Drivers that exceed the given incident point limit will be given a drive-through. A driver that accrues an additional 10 will be DQ.

2.5 Blue Flags and Multiclass Traffic

Blue flags between classes are advisory only. Drivers are not required to immediately yield position when receiving a blue flag from a faster class. Drivers of slower-class cars are expected to remain predictable and avoid unnecessarily hindering faster-class traffic. Drivers of faster-class cars are responsible for planning and executing passes safely. The responsibility for a safe pass remains with the overtaking vehicle.

Within the same class, blue flags are mandatory. Lapped cars must not interfere with lead-lap battles and are expected to facilitate a safe pass at the earliest available moment. Incidents involving blue-flag situations will receive heightened scrutiny from Race Control and the Competition Committee.

2.6 Netcode Incidents

Netcode is a known limitation of online racing and will be considered during incident reviews. If stewards determine that contact was solely the result of erroneous netcode, no penalty will normally be issued. If stewards determine that netcode merely amplified or slightly altered an otherwise avoidable incident, normal penalties may still apply. Drivers are expected to leave sufficient racing room to account for latency and connection variability.

2.7 Driver Conduct

All competitors are expected to race with respect for fellow members and in the spirit of fair competition. Actions that violate the intent of these rules, even if not specifically addressed within this document, may still be reviewed and penalized by the Competition Committee.

2.8 Rule Changes

The Competition Committee may amend these rules during a season when necessary to address competitive balance, league administration, or unforeseen circumstances. Any rule changes will be communicated to league members and will take effect on the date specified by the Competition Committee.

3. GT3/GTP League Specific Rules

3.1 Divisions

The GTP/GT3 league is a multiclass league consisting of those two cars, similar to IMSA without the LMP2. The GTP class will be a single division, but the GT3 class will be split into an AM and Pro division. For drivers who are not familiar to the league, GT3 AM will be anything below 2000IR, and Pro anything above. We understand that not all drivers’ IR are a realistic approximation of their skill, and adjustments can and will be made as they get familiar with the league. Driver classification will be determined at the start of each season by the Competition Committee and will remain fixed for the duration of that season unless otherwise announced.

3.2 Race Length

The league will consist of 12 races per season, with 8 of these races being sprints at 45 minutes, and 4 being endurance races at 90 minutes. Fuel will be capped at 50%/65% (GT3/GTP) to force a pit stop in the sprints. Endurance fuel will be set at 60% for GT3 and 65% for GTP to force a second stop. Race Control reserves the right to adjust fuel percentages if required due to changes in iRacing fuel consumption, vehicle balance updates, or race format adjustments.

3.3 Qualifying

Qualifying will be closed, consisting of 4 laps in a 10-minute session. If the league is at a track like Le Mans, time will be added to the session to allow for at least 2 full qualifying laps.

3.4 Manufacturer Lock

Drivers will be required to run the same car they start the season with.

Exception

We try to line iRacing official special events up with our league schedule. If you are participating in a special event, you may change your car during that week's league race if your special event team is running a different car. This applies only to in-class. You may not switch between GT3 and GTP.

The penalty for switching cars mid-season for any reason without prior permission of the admins will be a forfeiture of your highest point-earning week at the time you make the switch.

3.5 Cautions

Full-course cautions are not a standard part of GRR competition and will only be used in extraordinary circumstances. Race Control may deploy a full-course caution when an incident significantly impacts a substantial portion of the field and continuing under green conditions would create an unfair competitive situation. The decision to deploy a caution is final and not subject to protest.

3.6 First Lap Incidents

While generally more leeway is given to incidents on lap 1 due to the field being bunched up, all incidents on lap 1 will be scrutinized. If you are found to be responsible for contact that causes a crash on lap 1 due to gross over-aggression or negligence, you will be judged more harshly than other incidents. Racing incidents happen, but let's all remember we’re here for 45–90 minutes.

3.7 Start Procedure

While we’re very close to multiclass starts, iRacing isn’t quite there yet. The GTP field will be led by the pole sitter and they are responsible for starting the race between pace car exit and the green flag. The GT3 field will not go until the start/finish line. The GT3 pole sitter will leave an approximate 2-second gap to the GTP field, and start the race when they reach the line. Second place will wait on the leader to go. The rest of the GT3 field will go when the car in front of them accelerates. All GT3 drivers will stay in their line until they cross the start/finish line. Failure to do so will result in an EOL penalty for the next race.

5. Officials (In Session)

5.1 Officials (Live Admins)

While we aim to have live stewarding for all races, this is not normally a reality as people are volunteering their time for this and are not always available, or understandably would rather race than watch. If a live steward is available, racers may ask them to look at incidents by simply stating driver and lap in voice chat. No other information is allowed or needed. The live steward will make a determination of the severity of the incident, and either clear it, issue a time penalty, or issue a drive-through or park. Live stewarding decisions may be appealed, but due to the nature of the live component, it may not be possible for the CC to do anything other than clear the ICs accrued.

For races where there is not a live steward, incidents can be handled by the process below.

6. Protests and Protestable Infractions

6.1 Stewarding Philosophy

GRR recognizes that racing incidents are a natural part of motorsport. Not all contact, mistakes, or loss of control will result in a penalty. Stewards will review incidents based on the actions of the drivers involved, not solely the outcome of the incident.

When reviewing incidents, stewards may consider:
  • Driver positioning
  • Racing line
  • Predictability
  • Track conditions
  • Multiclass circumstances
  • Prior driver actions

The Competition Committee reserves the right to consider all available evidence when making a determination.

6.2 Protests

The admins will do a review of all incidents caught by iRacing’s incident tracker after the race. However, this will not catch everything, especially infractions such as blocking or blue flag infringement. If a driver feels that they are the victim of a rules infraction, they may file a protest by using the stewards ticket channel in the GRR Discord. Protests must be received within 24 hours of the end of the session in which the infraction took place or it will not be reviewed. Please include a clip of the incident with the ticket.

6.3 Procedural Infractions

6.3.1 Abuse of Track Limits

While the sim has built-in penalties for cutting the track, officials may also penalize drivers that use more than the accepted racing surface to gain either time or position. Examples are going off course to carry more speed off of a corner, or going to the apron to pass another driver on an oval.

6.3.2 Start/Restart Infractions

Drivers are to be sure to adhere to general start and restart rules, including maintaining tight formation, and not attempting to “jump” other drivers by creating gaps or getting runs. Further, drivers need to maintain their respective lanes until past the start/finish line when applicable.

6.3.3 iRacing Black Flag

We will not clear black flags given by iRacing, but if you receive a black flag that is due to another driver failing to follow iRacing and GRR rules, that driver may be subject to post-race protest and penalty.

6.3.4 Local Yellow Flags

Drivers approaching a visible incident are expected to make a reasonable effort to avoid involved vehicles. Continuing at full racing speed through an active accident scene may be considered a violation of this rule. A driver who unnecessarily worsens an incident through disregard for local yellow conditions may be subject to penalty.

6.3.5 Chat Abuse

Both voice and text chat must remain civil at all times. Offensive, abusive, or harassing communications directed toward another competitor may be grounds for penalty. Further, it is common courtesy to generally keep the airwaves clear during a race, and reserve constant communication to private channels.

6.4 Racecraft Infractions

These are infractions that take place on the track between drivers. All drivers are expected to maintain respect for their fellow competitors while on the track.

6.4.1 Blocking

Drivers may make one defensive move to protect their position.

Drivers may not:
  • Make multiple defensive moves.
  • Change direction in reaction to an attacking driver's movement.
  • Crowd another driver beyond track limits once overlap has been established.

Once meaningful overlap has been established, both drivers are expected to provide racing room.

6.4.2 Unsafe Entry

Entering back onto the racing surface in an unsafe manner, whether from pit lane or from off the track. Includes situations where cars are still on the racing surface but are stopped and/or pointing in the wrong direction and rejoin the racing.

6.4.3 Failure to Control Car

Drivers are expected to maintain control of their car while on the race track. Honest mistakes do happen and when that mistake directly and substantially impacts the race of others, that can be protested. Once a driver has clearly lost control, they are expected to hold the brakes until the car is done spinning to help make them more predictable for the cars around them; failure to do so will lead to a penalty.

6.4.4 Avoidable Contact

All drivers, even between classes, are expected to initiate and complete passes in a manner that leaves racing room for both drivers. Drivers are expected to initiate and complete overtaking maneuvers in a safe and controlled manner. A driver who causes avoidable contact through poor judgment, lack of awareness, excessive aggression, or failure to provide racing room may be subject to penalty. Avoidable contact that results in less serious outcomes may be subject to warning, with the option to increase to penalty if a pattern of behavior is established.

6.5 Cardinal Offenses

These are situations that, if found in violation, will result in major penalty and potentially removal from the league as a whole. Due to the uniqueness of these situations, the penalty for these will be decided by the Competition Committee to fit each situation. Accusations of this nature will not be taken lightly and should only be made in the truly extreme situation.

6.5.1 Retaliation

GRR has zero tolerance for retaliatory acts on track regardless of the situation. If you retaliate and intentionally wreck a driver during a race, you will be penalized.

6.5.2 Extreme Social/Chat Abuse

In cases of extreme and/or persistent social media abuse or chat behavior, the CC has the ability to treat it as a Cardinal Offense. This will not be tolerated and will result in discipline, up to and including removal from the league.

7. Determination of Fault

7.1 Determination of Fault

It is the responsibility of the Competition Committee and appointed stewards to review incidents submitted through the protest system and determine whether a rules violation occurred.

7.2 Racing Incident

A Racing Incident is an event in which no driver is determined to have committed a significant error, acted negligently, or violated league rules. Racing incidents are considered a normal part of motorsport and will not normally result in penalties.

7.3 Shared Responsibility

In situations where multiple drivers contribute to an incident, stewards may determine that responsibility is shared. Shared responsibility incidents may result in warnings, reduced penalties, or no penalties depending on the circumstances and severity of the incident.

7.4 Steward Discretion

Stewards may consider all available evidence when reviewing an incident, including driver positioning, racing line, predictability, track conditions, multiclass circumstances, and prior driver actions.

7.5 Repeated Behavior

Repeated minor incidents or rule violations may result in enhanced penalties even when the individual incidents would not normally warrant significant discipline.

8. Penalties

8.1 Ticket System

As stated above, certain actions or infractions are protestable. Racing is racing, and sometimes incidents will happen. However, even though this league is mostly for fun and bringing community together, we still do expect a certain level of racecraft to be maintained. That being said, if a driver feels that they have been on the receiving end of one of the actions listed above, they should file a protest using the ticketing system in Discord for the appropriate league channel. While staff makes an effort to be proactive, and we do watch all incidents after every race, we cannot catch every single possible protest.

8.2 IC Points and Penalties

Incident Credits (ICs) are used to track repeated racecraft and conduct issues throughout a season. ICs are assigned at the discretion of the Competition Committee following review of an incident. Not every protest or rules violation will necessarily result in the assignment of an IC. IC totals reset at the conclusion of each season. Not all incidents are malicious, but if they affect another driver’s race they must be addressed.

1 IC will be given for an incident determined to be at fault as outlined in Section 6. ICs are cumulative and will be reset at season's end.

Penalties for ICs are as follows:
  1. Verbal Warning
  2. Verbal Warning
  3. EOL
  4. EOL
  5. Start from pit
  6. Start from pit
  7. 1 Race suspension
  8. Season review by Competition Committee

In addition to the ICs listed above, being at fault for many incidents within a short span could also lead to action being taken.

  • 2 IC within a single race is an EOL (End of Line) for the next race.
  • 3 IC within a single race will be a 1 race suspension.

The penalties listed above will be served at the next race that the driver participates in.

8.3 Additional Penalties

The Competition Committee may assign penalties beyond the standard IC ladder when the severity of an incident warrants additional action.

Additional penalties may include, but are not limited to:
  • Verbal Warning
  • End of Line (EOL)
  • Start from Pit Lane
  • Points Deduction
  • Race Suspension
  • Season Disqualification

The severity of the incident, impact on other competitors, prior conduct, and any pattern of behavior may be considered when determining additional penalties.

9. Appeals

9.1 Appeals

If a driver feels that they have been improperly penalized, they have the option to file an appeal.

Post-race appeals must be submitted within 24 hours of receiving the penalty. Officials will respond with their acknowledgement to inform that they have received the request, and will respond with the decision, and as-needed amendment, inside of 72 hours. If a consensus is not able to be reached, the stewards may seek the opinion of an independent third party.

10. Points Systems

10.1 GTP/GT3 Points

The GT3/GTP league point system uses the same system as the IRL IMSA league. During any event that is labelled as an endurance race as noted on the schedule, points will be worth 1.5x value (rounded up) for the race.

We will allow one drop week per season. What this means is, out of the 12 weeks of the season, you will drop your worst points scored for the season, which could be a DNS/0. This will allow some breathing room for drivers that had to miss a week or got wrecked out.

The winner of each division will get to pick a track for the league to go to the following season. Picks must be made by the middle of week 13.

10.1.1 Fastest Lap

5 bonus points are awarded for the fastest lap.

10.1.2 Tie Breakers

In the event of a tie, the following tie breakers will be used in order:

  1. Most wins
  2. Most second place finishes
  3. Most poles
  4. Finishing position in final race
